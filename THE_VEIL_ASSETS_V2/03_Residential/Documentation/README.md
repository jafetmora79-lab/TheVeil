# THE VEIL - Pack 03: Greyhaven Residential

These are the houses, interiors and furniture for Greyhaven's residential streets.

* Every house is fully enterable and walkable in first person.
* The player's home is staged for the opening scene: Friday evening, wake up, take the car keys, walk out to the sedan.
* The houses are fitted into the 25 Pack 03 building zones of the Pack 02 town layout. The Pack 02 street network, zones and placements are unchanged.

Everything here is generated by Python scripts in `Source_Scripts/`. The output is deterministic, so the same code always produces the same files:

```bash
python3 03_Residential/Source_Scripts/build_residential.py
```

```bash
python3 03_Residential/Source_Scripts/validate_residential.py --gltf-validator <dir> --godot <Godot_4.3> --godot-project <scratch>
```

Validation results are in `VALIDATION_REPORT.md`. Every model's dimensions, triangle counts, materials and interactive parts are listed in `MODEL_MANIFEST.md` / `.json`. The house-to-lot assignment is in `HOUSE_LOTS.json`.

### House frame

The local frame is the same for every house:

* x runs across the frontage, centred on the zone.
* The street is toward **−Z**, with the zone front line at z = 0.
* Lot level is y = 0.

`Greyhaven_Residential.tscn` places each house at world y = 0.15, the Pack 02 lot level.

## Player home (`Player_Home/Player_Home.tscn`)

The player home is a normal, safe, lived-in single-storey house with no supernatural elements:

* **Bedroom:** an unmade bed, an alarm clock reading 6:47 PM, a calendar with Friday circled, a desk with a PC, posters and clothes.
* **Entrance hall and living room:** a coat rack holding the car keys (`INT_Pickup_CarKeys`), a CRT TV, a sofa and a recliner.
* **Other rooms:** kitchen/dining, bathroom, laundry, and a garage with a workbench and storage.

The furniture is instanced piece by piece, so every drawer, cabinet door and pickup is a live node.

The scene includes these markers:

* `Markers/Spawn_WakeUp`: beside the bed. Forward (−Z) faces away from the bed.
* `Markers/Pickup_CarKeys`: on the coat rack.
* `Markers/Sedan_Driveway`: the Pack 02 journey point `2_SEDAN_ON_DRIVEWAY`. Forward faces the street; the driver's door is on −X. The car itself belongs to Pack 04.
* `Markers/WakePath/WP_*`: a furniture-aware path from bed → bedroom door → hall → living room → front door → porch steps → walkway → driver's door. The same list is stored in `metadata/veil_wake_path`.

Lighting:

* There are warm OmniLights in the hall, living room and kitchen, at the lamps and on the porch. None casts shadows.
* The bedroom ceiling light starts off, so the bedside lamp is the only light at wake-up.

## Interaction conventions

Moving parts are separate nodes placed at their pivot. Architectural doors, sliding doors and garage doors have their own convex collider as a child, so moving the pivot moves the collision. Furniture doors, lids and drawers sit inside their carcass collision hull.

| Prefix | Motion | Pivot / axis |
|---|---|---|
| `INT_Door_*` | hinge, `open_deg` | hinge line, local Y; leaf extends +X |
| `INT_SlidingDoor_*` | slide `distance` | local X |
| `INT_GarageDoor_*` | slide `distance` | up (local Y), sectional |
| `INT_Window_*` (sash) | slide `distance` | up (local Y) |
| `INT_Drawer_*` | slide | out along local +Z |
| `INT_CabinetDoor_*` | hinge | cabinet edge, local Y |
| `INT_LightSwitch_<room>` | toggle | child `Toggle` flips; pairs with marker `Light_<room>` |
| `INT_Pickup_*` | pickup | e.g. car keys |

The motion parameters are written in two places:

* **glTF node `extras`:** `{"veil_interact": "hinge" | "slide" | "toggle" | "pickup", ...}`.
* **`.tscn` metadata on the scene root:** `metadata/veil_interactables`, a list of `{node, pos, veil_interact, ...}` dictionaries.

**Godot 4.3 does not import glTF node extras as metadata.** This was verified in the engine. Game code should therefore read `veil_interactables` from the `.tscn` root:

* `node` is a unique node name; resolve it with `find_child(name, true, false)`.
* In `Player_Home.tscn`, `node` can instead be a path relative to the root, for example `Furniture/CoatRack_Wall_01/RES_CoatRack_Wall/INT_Pickup_CarKeys`.

The validator checks in Godot that every entry resolves to a node.

## Collision

* **Houses:** one trimesh (`*_Col-colonly`) for walls, floors, ceilings, roofs, porches and steps.
* **Stairs:** collide as smooth ramps under 45° (Godot's default `floor_max_angle`). Every storey, half-level, basement and attic stair is walkable without step-up code.
* **Openings:** door openings are clear. Every hinged door was checked fully open (95°): its leaf never blocks a hall, a stair foot or another doorway, and the capsule walk runs with all doors open. Garage floors, which are lower than the house floor, have automatic interior steps plus ramp collision.
* **Furniture:** convex boxes (`-convcolonly`), one per major mass.
* **Furnishing batches:** keep only the collision of floor-standing pieces, merged into one trimesh. Wall-mounted decor and small items placed on surfaces have no collision.
* **Lot connections:** visual only. They are 1–2 cm slabs on the Pack 02 lot ground, which already has collision.

## Materials and textures

Materials are reused wherever possible:

* **Pack 01:** siding, brick, roofing, floorboards, carpet, linoleum, tiles, wallpapers, drywall, fabrics, metals, glass.
* **Pack 02:** door and trim paints, the font.

Pack 03 adds 16 textures (64 px/m, nearest filtering, lossless):

* Plaid, quilt and gingham fabrics, Formica and porcelain.
* Mirror, harvest-gold enamel, wood panelling, insulation, pegboard and lattice.
* Garage-door panels, shutters, a braided rug and a lampshade.
* A 256 px **detail atlas**: CRT screen, clock and alarm faces, calendar, book spines, photos, appliance panels, fridge notes, cereal box, light switch, poster, newspaper and PC screen.

Each GLB embeds glTF PBR materials for other tools. Its `.import` file maps every material to the shared `.tres` files via `use_external`.

## Lot fitting

The solver in `placement.py` reads `02_Town/Town_Layout/greyhaven_layout.json` and the Pack 02 manifest. It never writes to them. For each zone it picks the mirror and a lateral shift that satisfy these rules:

* **Exterior walls stay inside the zone.** The zone line is treated as the wall centreline, so the outer wall face may sit ≤ 0.12 m past it.
* **Garages line up with the Pack 02 driveway** (within ≤ 0.35 m). For garage-less houses, nothing low sits on the driveway corridor.
* **Nothing touches a Pack 02 placement:** trees, bushes, flower beds, mailboxes, water meters or fences.
* **Front doors face the street.**
* **Each front door gets a walkway.** A concrete path is routed from the front steps to the driveway around Pack 02 props.

The street network and Pack 02 placements are unchanged.

|---|---|
| PLAYER_HOME | `RES_PlayerHome` |
| FARMHOUSE (Quarry Rd) | `RES_House_Farmhouse` |
| MILL_HOUSE (Mill Rd) | `RES_House_MillWorker` |
| HOUSE_01–23 | Ranch ×6, Two-storey ×4, Cottage ×4, Bungalow ×3, Mill-worker ×2, Split-level ×2, Abandoned ×1 |

The full table is in `HOUSE_LOTS.json` and `MODEL_MANIFEST.md`. No two neighbouring lots share the same facade.

## Godot

* **Renderer:** Godot 4 Compatibility.
* **Import:** every GLB has an `.import` file with external materials and shadow meshes. Textures are lossless with mipmaps and nearest filtering.
* **Scenes:** instance a house's `.tscn` (house + furnishing + metadata). Use the plain GLB if you will furnish it yourself.

## Performance notes

* **Typical house:** about 5–11 k visual triangles, plus 1.7–5.8 k for its furnishing batch. Each costs one draw call per material (about 20–40 surfaces for a house).
* **Whole town:** all 25 houses in town total roughly 270 k visual triangles; see `VALIDATION_REPORT.md` for the exact figure.
* **Background houses:** use the batched furnishing, whose moving parts are baked closed.
* **Player home:** the only house with individually instanced furniture, so its node count is higher.
* **Lights:** no shadows, and only the player home has lights.

For distant lots, consider hiding `House/<name>_Interior` and the `Furnishing` node, or swapping in an exterior-only LOD, behind a visibility range. LODs are not generated.

## Known limitations

* **Nothing has been rendered.** Visual quality, texture alignment and silhouettes have not been checked by eye. The validator only checks geometry, files and engine behaviour.
* **glTF extras are ignored by Godot 4.3.** Interaction data comes from the `.tscn` metadata, as described above. The motion itself (animating hinges and slides) is left to gameplay code; nothing here scripts it.
* **Mirrored furnishing** reuses the un-mirrored models, rotated into place. Handed pieces, such as an L-shaped desk, do not flip.
* **The furniture placer** drops or substitutes a few optional pieces where a room is too tight. These are listed under `furnishing_notes` in `MODEL_MANIFEST.json`.
* **Some grid-planner legs are reported as warnings.** The 0.1 m grid is conservative and cannot route a few legs (for example around the farmhouse attic eaves). The capsule walk in Godot passes them; the report lists them as warnings.
* **The combined town scene hides the Pack 02 zone preview boxes** through an override. The Pack 02 scene file is unchanged, and its zone labels stay visible.
* **There are no basements or attics** in most houses. Only the two-storey house has a basement and only the farmhouse has a walk-in attic, by design.
